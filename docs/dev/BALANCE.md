# Original simulation coefficients

All values describe fictional game tradeoffs; they do not predict real processor performance, prices, LLM quality or training costs. Tuning data is in `packages/content/src/{balance,era,hardware,devices,research,management,ai,events}.ts`. Engine code retains mathematical identities, byte/unit conversions, tick periods and input safety bounds. Save version1 keeps historical cached product metrics; updated research affects subsequent designs.

- Normal starts at ¥1,000,000,000, hard at ¥500,000,000 (see economy version 3 below). Ten offices have real-world monthly rents (¥200,000 garage to ¥35,000,000 campus), cost ten months of rent to move into, staff capacity4 per level and research slots1 every two levels. Factory capacity200 per level per day is shared across lines, rotating priority daily. Salaries follow skill; founder work is shared among research, hardware and model preparation.
- Hardware performance combines chosen specs, costs and power; price elasticity1.5 penalizes expensive products. Weekly demand includes seeded variance, reliability, awareness/reputation, product age, competing available supply and expiring market events. Hard demand ×0.8, hard rivals ×1.5; CPU demand1,400 preserves a profitable default180-day research/production/reinvestment fixture. This is a tested path, not proof that every company remains solvent.
- Research is split per specification since economy version 4 (see below). Each slider names the branch that widens it and a starting limit (`unlock.from`); the open fraction is that branch's stage progress (`level/50`) raised to0.6, snapped to the slider step, so early stages open noticeably and the full catalog range needs stage50. `lowerIsBetter` fields (process nm, latency, response time) lower their minimum instead. Saved products keep their stored specs; the ranges apply to new designs only.
- Cooling (W) is a spec of CPUs, GPUs, PCs and laptops. A design whose power exceeds its cooling is rejected; a PC/laptop's power includes the optional PSU's conversion loss. Cooling adds unit cost (CPU¥50/W, GPU¥40/W, PC¥30/W, laptop¥60/W, divided by the research frontier growth) and up to +0.02 reliability at50% headroom. Older saves without a cooling value load unchanged.
- Cash is integer and nonnegative. Optional spending rejects insufficient funds; mandatory expenses record unpaid debt. Income repays overdue debt first. Loans are separate, capped and accrue monthly interest. Revenue, operating expenses and capex remain separate cumulative ledgers. Monthly history retains240 snapshots; the first visible truncated flow is unknown rather than fabricated.
- Production charges cash when stock is made; selling credits revenue. Product profit is gross margin using per-unit manufacturing costs, not net company profit. Own parts are consumed once; assembly pays packaging/vendor parts only. Bundled OS revenue is allocated from the device's sale price and never generates a second company receipt. Inventory is finite and excluded from cash. Acquisitions transfer valued stock/cash/debt once.
- Training uses `6 × active parameters × tokens`, efficiency0.4 multiplied by network/cooling, MoE overhead1.15 and a context overhead. All weights use total parameters for memory; training estimates16 bytes/parameter. This lower-bound allocation excludes sophisticated optimizer/sharding schedules. Preparation7 days shares staff/founder work independently of cluster physical time.
- Inference adds total-parameter weights, `2 × layers × hidden × context × batch × 2-byte` KV memory (grouped attention ×0.25), and10% weight reserve. Four/eight/sixteen-bit speed factors1.5/1.2/1 have quality penalties0.08/0.01/0. Scaled utilization and saturating five-score curves cap scores99. MoE may reduce compute while its full weights still occupy memory.
- AI costs include data, electricity¥20/kWh, operations¥35/GPU-hour and three-year equipment depreciation. Own stock has zero new purchase payment but retains asset value in depreciation. Training/paid hosting exclusively reserve a cluster; open weights use no hosting resources and gain brand visibility. API/subscriptions are capacity bounded, fees3% and refunds2%; operating debt stops service. Competing quotes depend on rival progress and player prices. Commercial models improve GPU/server demand; private unequipped models do not.
- Four original monthly events change demand or production by5%, expire after30 days, and use the saved PRNG. Every engine command and tick is deterministic across save continuation. The365-day fixture verifies bounded finite state and equal replay; it is not a statistical balance study.

## Technology eras and real-world money (economy version 2)

- Every game starts on 1 January 2000 (`eraBalance` in `era.ts`; new-game input accepts only 2000). Slider starting limits (`unlock.from`) and defaults are year-2000 technology (one 0.8GHz core on 180nm, 32MB VRAM, 128MB SDR RAM, 20GB HDD, 768p monitor); absolute bounds were year-2100 technology (4,096 cores, 0.1nm, 4TB VRAM, 16TB RAM, 4PB storage) until economy version 5 added the year-2200 tier (see below). Sliders spanning several orders of magnitude open geometrically (`log`) over a list of real-world steps (`values`).
- A category's technology year is `2000 + 100 × mean stage share` of the branches that widen its sliders (shown in the designer and research screen). Option lists open by year: memory SDR and DDR (2000), DDR2 2004, DDR3 2007, DDR4 2014, DDR5 2021, then fictional DDR6–DDR9 (2030, 2042, 2058, 2078), PSU semi/full modular 2004/2008.
- Unit costs use each value divided by its field's frontier growth, so the same specification gets cheaper as research advances (mass production follows the frontier); performance and power use the designed values. CPU/GPU power scale with the process node (`(nm/180)^0.6`), GPU units with exponent0.88.
- The market moves with the calendar: `marketReference(kind, day)` is the performance of the mainstream design at market share `years/100` (rivals build the same designs), used by demand, reviews, price ranges and benchmark loads. A product therefore ages even when nobody launches a rival. Rivals research one level per ~100 weeks up to level50.
- Money is real-world yen: catalog costs, prices, salaries, offices, loans, events and achievements are ×100 the version-1 scale, OS licensing/design ×200, generic year-2000 parts ~¥7,000–¥35,000 at 1.8× their manufacturing cost. AI service prices (API ¥/1M tokens, subscriptions) were already real-world and kept; API demand rose ×100 so revenue matches the new costs. GPU throughput is `1 GFLOPS × (score/1600)^2`, so useful model training starts with 2030s-era GPUs.
- Saves from economy version 1 are migrated once on load (`packages/sim/src/migrate.ts`): money ×100, calendar restarted at 2000, rival levels capped to the calendar; designed product specs and stored metrics are kept.

## Real-world company costs (economy version 3)

- Development, salaries and offices are real-world amounts instead of ×100 gameplay values. Design costs: CPU and GPU ¥300M, storage ¥100M, monitor ¥50M, motherboard ¥30M, RAM ¥20M, PSU ¥15M, PC/laptop ¥30M, OS ¥1B (support ¥10M/month). Monthly salary is `¥200,000 + skill × ¥5,000` (skill50 = ¥450,000, frugal ×0.8); hiring pays `skill × ¥30,000`, training ¥300,000. Factory upgrades start at ¥30M with ¥2M/month per level; distribution ¥20M and ¥2M/month.
- To keep these costs payable, the company starts with venture-scale capital (¥1B normal, ¥500M hard), demand is ×20 the version-2 values and factory capacity ×10 (200 units/day per level). Loan caps are ¥500M/¥250M. Achievement thresholds and the hard-mode goal (¥1 trillion) scale with them, and mass production means 10,000 units.
- Rivals start with ¥2B, pay their focus category's design cost, ¥50M per research level and ¥15M/month, and build 120 units/day toward a 1,000-unit stock.
- Version-2 saves are migrated once: the company and each independent rival receive the difference in starting capital, and staff salaries are re-priced with the new formula. Version-1 saves pass through both steps.
- Evidence (seeded runs of a player who designs and redesigns one CPU every year): normal cash ¥1B → ~¥760M after the first design → ~¥1.9B after six years; a GPU company ends near ¥1B; hard mode ends near ¥700M (CPU) and ¥280M (GPU). The rival focused on the player's category slowly loses cash. Research still outpaces the calendar (about 2020-era technology by 2006).

## Per-specification research, production cost, power and reviews (economy version 4)

- Every research branch improves exactly one thing (`packages/content/src/tech-catalog.ts`). Hardware: one foundation (`<category>-compute-1`, unlocks development) plus one branch per design slider (`cpu-cores-N`, `cpu-clock-N`, `cpu-l2Cache-N`, …, 50 stages of two technology years) and one short branch per option list (memory generation, SMT, PSU cables, monitor panel; one stage per two years up to the last option). PC/laptop: case cooling. OS: quality, compatibility, support and one stage per extra feature. AI: the six efficiency branches keep their effects; model size, layers, hidden size, context and data sliders each have their own branch. Stage1 needs only the foundation, later stages the previous stage. Per-category cost weights keep researching every branch about as expensive as the four shared branches of version3. The old cost/power/reliability branches are gone; costs now fall through the frontier model below instead.
- Merged specs were split like real products: motherboard USB ports, storage ports and LAN speed (instead of a feature count), storage read and write speed, monitor size, panel type (TN/VA/IPS 2004/OLED 2018/MicroLED 2032), response time and brightness, and CPU L1/L2/L3 cache (the merged total cache is accepted for stored products only).
- Production cost: CPU/GPU logic is priced per process node, `(nm/180)^1.2 × (0.5 + 1.0 × fresh)` where `fresh = 0.5^(nodes behind the research frontier)`, so the newest node costs a premium and mature nodes get cheap; other values cost `value / growth^(1 - frontierCost)`. Seeded results: a frontier CPU costs about ¥20k (2000), ¥104k (2025), ¥367k (2100); a frontier GPU ¥22k, ¥58k, ¥159k; the year-2000 default spec falls to ~¥8.5k (CPU) / ~¥4k (GPU). Version3 kept every frontier near ¥16–20k for the whole century.
- Power: energy per operation scales with `(nm/180)^1` and below 4nm additionally with `(nm/4)^1.2`. A frontier GPU that fits its research-limited cooler draws ~25W (2000), ~420W (2025), ~800W (2100) of 1,500W; a frontier CPU ~40W, ~270W, ~470W of 1,000W. Version3 reached 33,565W for a full-research GPU.
- Reviews are no longer capped at 100: each axis above 100 is softened to `100 + 50·ln(1 + (v−100)/50)`, stars stay at most five, and demand gains a log2 bonus above 100 (`reviewBalance.overflowScale`, `demandOverflow`).
- Discontinuing a product stops its line, removes its deal offers, sells the retail stock at 50% of its price and excludes it from new PCs, OS bundles, demand and rival comparisons.
- Rivals have fan bases (3,000 at the start, +1% of units sold, −0.5% per week). Acquiring a rival transfers its whole fan base.
- Borrowing limit: the larger of ¥500M/¥250M and 50%/30% (normal/hard) of the company value, rounded down to ¥1M. Company value already nets out the loan, so borrowing never raises its own limit.
- Bulk-deal offers stop interactive time and open a full-screen event until answered; batch simulation is unchanged.
- Version3 saves are migrated before validation: each new branch receives `ceil(mean old share × 50)` stages of the shared branches that used to widen it (so no designed range shrinks), foundations, appearance and AI efficiency ids are kept, and rivals receive the starting fan base.

Adjustment evidence: default CPU business regression after independent rivals corrected with demand60→70; numerical purpose comparisons use asymmetric parts because equally balanced components may have equal rounded purpose scores. All conclusions are limited to the recorded fixtures; long-term playtesting by humans remains desirable.

## Year-2200 research tier, sockets and line-up management (economy version 5)

- **Advanced tier.** Every hardware slider branch has 100 stages instead of 50 (`maxResearchLevels`); stage 50 still reaches the year-2100 bound (`unlock.base`) and stages 51–100 open the final bound (`min`/`max`), so the technology year runs to 2200 (`eraBalance.endYear`, share 2). The open fraction is `s^0.6` up to share 1 and `1 + (s−1)^0.6` beyond. Stage costs keep the version-4 weights (`min(levels, 50)` in the weight span), so stages 1–50 cost exactly what they did; later stages keep growing with the stage number. AI and OS sliders keep 50 stages. New option steps: process nodes 0.07/0.05/0.035/0.025nm, DDR10 (2120) and DDR11 (2165), a holographic monitor panel (2150; mainstream 2175).
- **Final bounds (year 2100 → 2200).** CPU 4,096 → 16,384 cores, 10 → 25GHz, IPC 10 → 25, 0.1 → 0.025nm, L3 64GB → 256GB, 16 → 64 memory channels, cooler 1,000 → 2,000W. GPU 65,536 → 262,144 units, 6 → 15GHz, 4TB → 16TB VRAM, 1 → 4PB/s, cooler 1,500 → 3,000W. RAM 16TB → 64TB, latency 1 → 0.25ns. Storage 4PB → 20PB, 2 → 8TB/s. PSU 3,000 → 12,000W, 99 → 99.9%. Monitor 100 → 150 inch, 16K → 64K, 2,000 → 8,000Hz, 0.01 → 0.002ms, 10,000 → 40,000 nits. PC/laptop case cooling 2,500 → 5,000W / 600 → 1,200W. Every slider except percentages, screen size, slot count and coolers grows at least 2.5× (`tests/advanced-tier.test.ts`).
- **Power and cost stay bounded.** Below 0.1nm logic cost grows again by `(nm/0.1)^0.8` (`hardwareBalance.node.deepProcess`, `deepCostExponent`), so the frontier does not become free. Frontier designs that fit their own cooler (cores/units halved until they do) measured at share 1 / 2: CPU 467W of 1,000W → 884W of 2,000W, ¥367k → ¥421k; GPU 801W of 1,500W → 1,284W of 3,000W, ¥159k → ¥120k. Frontier performance at 2200 relative to 2100: CPU ×65, GPU ×4, motherboard ×6, RAM ×20, storage ×5, monitor ×43, PSU ×4. No design comes near the version-3 33,565W problem.
- **Market and rivals continue to 2200.** The calendar market reaches share 2 in year 2200, generic parts are released every five years until 2200, and rivals research up to level 99 (`rivalShare(level) = (level−1)/49`, capped at 2).
- **CPU sockets.** A socket caps a new CPU's power draw and memory channels. Industry sockets are free and appear on the calendar: Atlas 120W/2ch and Nova 180W/4ch (2000), Orion 250W/8ch (2045), Lyra 400W/16ch (2080), Polaris 800W/32ch (2100), Sirius 1,200W/64ch (2165). Rivals and generic parts use the mainstream one of their year (Atlas from 2000, Nova 2022, Orion 2049, Lyra 2084, Polaris 2105, Sirius 2168); every year's mainstream CPU fits it (`tests/sockets.test.ts`). The `cpu-socket-1` research (weight 10) lets a company develop its own socket: power up to its CPU cooler limit and channels up to its memory-channel limit, ¥100M × `(W/120)^0.3 × (ch/2)^0.2` (rounded to ¥1M) and 20 units of engineering work shared with product development. Motherboards for a company socket cost ¥10/W and ¥300/channel more than Atlas; industry sockets add nothing. CPU and motherboard of a PC must share a socket, as before.
- **Rival line-ups (#41).** A rival keeps its three newest models on sale (`rivalBalance.activeModels`). Older ones are retired: stock is cleared at the clearance share of their price (recorded as rival sales), production stops, and they leave demand, competitor pressure, price comparisons and acquisitions. A player PC that still uses a retired part gets a notice.
- **Research shortcuts (#42).** `researchBranch` researches several consecutive stages of one branch in one command; each stage is checked and paid like a single research, and any failure rejects the whole command. The research screen offers the affordable run of stages, recommends branches whose limit the newest design already uses (or whose cooler it nearly fills), and slider limits in the designers link to their branch.
- **Statistics (#43).** Monthly history stores cumulative sales, revenue and profit per product kind (`history[].kinds`); the statistics screen draws monthly units and revenue per kind and shows unit cost, gross margin and a discontinuation hint (below cost, or no sale for 90 days, from `lastSaleDay`).
- **Old models (#44).** A product's unit cost is fixed when it is designed (manufacturing charges `unitCost`), so an old model does not get cheaper to build as research advances. In seeded runs a firm that refreshes its CPU every four years ends far ahead of one that keeps selling its first model (`tests/sales-stats.test.ts`); no cost change was needed.
- **Migration.** Version-4 saves load as version 5: rivals retire everything but their three newest models once (`toVersion5`). Research ids, products and stored specs are unchanged; new fields (`sockets`, `lastSaleDay`, `kinds`) default to empty.
